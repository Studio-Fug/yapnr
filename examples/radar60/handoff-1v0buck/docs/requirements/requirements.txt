ENGINEERING REQUIREMENTS / FEASIBILITY
Design requirements for a
commodity phased array radar
Status: proposal and feasibility assessment, 2026-10-02. Proposed yapnr demonstration; no
hardware has been built or simulated for this study. All performance requirements are targets.
Calculations are first-order estimates, and costs are allocations unless a price source is identified.
Decision: develop a 60 GHz FMCW radar with custom PCB antennas and feed networks, using
the external-antenna TI IWR6843 as the reference radio. A materials BOM below $100 at 10,000 units
per year is plausible. The proposed nominal allocation is $70, with a $14 contingency. The credible
first application is low-speed autonomous ground vehicles and robots at approximately 0.5-20 m. A
highway perception radar, reliable pedestrian detection in all conditions, and automotive qualification are
separate projects.
This document follows yapnr's design-document structure and adds stable requirement IDs and
verification criteria. The inspected main-branch snapshot is 8dfcf009e808208649387e00d6c3a053d9959ef2.
No file literally named rules_requirements was present in that snapshot. The RF coupon and FEA design
documents provide the closest local precedent. [R1-R4]
1. Goals and scope
1.1 What the demonstration must prove
The demonstration shall start with electrical and radiation-pattern requirements, select commodity
components, synthesize copper RF structures, place and route the complete board, and evaluate the
resulting coupled design. It shall show the physical manufactured radar and a reproducible comparison
against a conventional design on the same stackup.
SYS-01 - Cost boundary. The recurring materials BOM shall be less than $100 USD at a stated
10,000-unit annual purchase volume, with a design target of $85 or less. It includes the radio, processing,
memory, clocks, power conversion, interface components, PCB copper antennas, radome, connectors
and mounting hardware. It excludes assembly, calibration labor, yield loss, certification, development
equipment, engineering and host vehicle. These exclusions shall be priced separately; an external
computer needed to perform radar processing must be included in the BOM. Verification: dated supplier
quotations and an exact-part BOM at gate G5.
SYS-02 - Commodity construction. Use catalog ICs, ordinary SMT assembly and a commercially
offered PCB process. No custom silicon, antenna-in-package radio, hand-tuned wire structures, exotic RF
connector in each shipped unit, or external SDR is allowed in the demonstration BOM. A low-loss RF
laminate is acceptable commodity hardware. Verification: manufacturing package and supplier process
review.
SYS-03 - Application. The initial operational domain is a ground vehicle moving at up to 3 m/s, in a
controlled test area, with radar targets at 0.5-20 m and radial target speeds up to 4.5 m/s. Initial
environmental characterization covers dry conditions and 0-50 degrees C ambient. This is an additional
perception sensor, not an approved sole braking sensor. Verification: a published test-domain declaration
and recorded test runs.
SYS-04 - Scope of automation. yapnr shall generate and route every external RF interconnect,
antenna feed and designated inverse-designed structure. The radio's internal PLL, mixers, LNAs and
ADCs remain vendor-designed blocks with documented interface models. A successful result
demonstrates the external signal chain and board design; it does not claim to have synthesized the radio
silicon. Verification: generated geometry provenance from requirements to final board.

SYS-05 - Integration envelope. Target a sensor envelope no larger than 100 x 70 x 25 mm, powered
from a regulated 5 V input with a 2 A source allowance, and no more than 4 W average consumption in
the released 20 Hz profile. Output timestamp, mode, range, radial speed, azimuth, SNR/confidence and
diagnostic status for each detection. Verify measured peak/average power, processing latency and
junction temperature at the maximum declared ambient. An automotive battery/transient front end is
outside this initial regulated-input domain and must be costed if added.
1.2 Non-goals for the first release
 - Highway-speed autonomous driving, 100-200 m guaranteed detection, or dense 3D imaging.
 - Automotive qualification or a system functional-safety claim inferred from the IC's qualification.
 - Simultaneous fine angular resolution, narrow transmit beams and a large field of view from a handful of
 RF channels.
- A production-ready design generated solely from nominal FR-4 data.
 - Replacing measured RF validation with KiCad DRC or replacing DRC with an electromagnetic result.

Terminology matters. The formal ISM designation near 60 GHz is 61-61.5 GHz, not all of 57-71 GHz.

contained in 60-64 GHz. [R8, R9]

2.2 Radio selection
ARCH-01 - Reference radio. Use an external-antenna IWR6843, with the exact purchasable suffix and
silicon revision frozen at G1. Its documented capabilities include 60-64 GHz operation, three TX channels,
four RX channels, 6-bit TX phase control, complex receive conversion and integrated processing. TI's
published comparison lists approximately $18.963 at 1,000 units; this is a price anchor, not a
10,000-unit quote. The BOM allocates $22. [R5-R7]
Use the IWR6843 because it supports an actual transmit phase-steering experiment as well as receive
beamforming. The lower-cost IWRL6432 is a viable digital/MIMO radar alternative, but its binary TX phase
control is not a substitute for arbitrary phase steering. The newer IWRL6844 also documents binary
phase control; more channels alone do not establish equivalent beamforming capability. Do not silently
substitute either part. [R15, R16]
24 GHz fallback. Infineon's BGT24LTR11 has accessible analog I/Q outputs, and the manufacturer
demonstrates software-controlled FMCW. It is attractive for a 1D range demo or a switched-beam
antenna. A true multibeam system additionally needs RF switching, a beam-forming network, baseband
conversion and calibration; its one TX and one RX do not create a phased array by themselves. This
fallback is useful if 60 GHz fabrication/metrology fails the early gates, with the range-separation
requirement explicitly relaxed to approximately 1 m for a guarded sweep. [R17]
Sub-GHz conclusion. An SX1262 may supply an experimental CW source, but its normal packet
receiver does not expose the coherent multi-channel sample stream required here. Its 500 kHz LoRa
bandwidth corresponds to roughly 300 m ideal radar resolution if used as the observation bandwidth. A
separate coherent receiver and stepped-frequency processing would be a different radio architecture.
The revised baseline therefore has no LoRa dependency; telemetry can be added separately if needed.
[R18; calculation]

ARCH-02 - Two explicit modes. Implement:
 1. Perception mode: time-division multiplex the three TX channels, receive all four channels, estimate
 range and Doppler, compensate target motion between TX slots, then form angle estimates from the
actual virtual-array geometry.
2. Phased-transmit demonstration mode: excite all three TX channels coherently, set phase
 progressions for -30, 0 and +30 degrees, and collect four RX channels. Measure the far-field beam shift
and show the effect on target illumination. Each beam gets a separate coherent processing interval.
These modes shall have separate performance reports. They do not simultaneously deliver the same
MIMO aperture, Doppler resolution and transmit gain. Verification: firmware configuration plus measured
mode-specific patterns and detections.
ARCH-03 - Three-TX supply mode. The board shall implement the documented 1 V RF/PA LDO-bypass
arrangement for three simultaneous TX, including the required PA supply connection, sequencing,
current capacity and ripple limits. Budget for the documented 2.5 A peak on that rail; average board
power is a different quantity. Verify the exact revision's data sheet and firmware controls before
schematic release. Verification: schematic review and rail-current/ripple measurements in the worst
transmit state. [R6, sections 7.6-7.7 and 8.3.1]

ANT-01 - Steering geometry. Start with azimuth TX positions 0, d, 2d and RX positions 0, d, 2d, 3d,
where d = c/(2*64 GHz) = 2.342 mm. TX and RX banks may have a common physical translation and
vertical separation. Their azimuth axes must remain parallel. The spacing is referenced to the highest
design frequency. Verification: exact exported phase centers and full-wave embedded-element patterns.
This deliberately favors clean transmit steering. The twelve TX/RX pair measurements have only six
distinct azimuth sum positions, with multiplicities 1, 2, 3, 3, 2, 1. They are not twelve independent
uniformly spaced virtual elements. Equalizing the multiplicities affects noise; angle processing shall use
the actual covariance and measured manifold. The expected resolution is coarse: roughly 18 degrees
ideal broadside beamwidth for a uniformly weighted six-position equivalent, with a 25-degree
two-target acceptance target.
Spacing TX phase centers by 4d would instead produce twelve distinct MIMO positions with the same four
RX positions. That is a separate layout option. Its sparse transmit array creates grating-lobe problems
when all TX transmit coherently, so it cannot inherit the baseline's steering claims. The design shall never
advertise both benefits without demonstrating how it achieves them. [R11; geometry calculation]
ANT-02 - Radiation targets. Across 60.3-63.8 GHz, require return loss of at least 10 dB per external
antenna port, nominal per-column realized broadside gain of at least 5 dBi, and useful azimuth coverage
of at least +/-45 degrees. In phased-transmit mode, main-lobe pointing shall be within 5 degrees of each
commanded direction after calibration. No unintended lobe within the forward scan sector may exceed
the selected main lobe. Treat total main-beam efficiency, cross-polarization, sidelobes and scan loss as
measured outputs, not just antenna matching. Verification: embedded-element simulation and chamber
patterns with the final radome.

4.3 First-order sensitivity check
Use the monostatic approximation:
Pr = EIRP * Gr * lambda^2 * sigma / ((4*pi)^3 * R^4 * L).
For an illustrative conservative indoor profile, assume 12 dBm EIRP, 5 dBi realized gain at one RX port, 62
GHz, a 1 square meter target at 20 m, and 3 dB additional two-way environmental/radome loss. The
received power is approximately -117.3 dBm per receiver. Feed loss is already included in realized
gain and is not subtracted a second time.
With an assumed 12 dB receiver noise figure and Hann-window equivalent noise bandwidth 1.5/64 us =
23.44 kHz, thermal noise per range bin is approximately -118.3 dBm. Ideal coherent accumulation of 20
chirps adds 13.0 dB, and combining four RX channels adds up to 6.0 dB. After a further assumed 3 dB
processing/window allowance, the illustrative result is 17.0 dB SNR. This deliberately credits only one
TX's coherent sequence; extra duplicate MIMO measurements are not counted as free aperture gain.

At 10 m, a 0.1 square meter target gives approximately 19.0 dB under the same assumptions. Halving
range adds about 12 dB; reducing RCS by ten times costs 10 dB. These estimates support a prototype,

5. RF copper and fabrication requirements
RF-01 - Stackup as a contract. Freeze a supplier-specific stackup, RF laminate, thickness distribution,
copper finish/roughness, etch tolerance, mask openings and via geometry. Start with a thin low-loss RF
layer over a solid reference plane and ordinary lower layers for power/digital routing. Short-path
characterized FR-4 is a cost-down experiment, not an interchangeable substitute. TI's RF layout guide
provides a manufacturing reference; exact dimensions must come from this design's stackup. [R12]
RF-02 - Copper components. Synthesize at least two meaningful classes: the antenna radiator/column
and a distributed feed component such as a two-way divider plus matching transition. Their emitted
copper shall be the copper fabricated. Optimizing a picture, then manually redrawing it, is insufficient.
Each structure shall carry ports, reference planes, frequency masks, a fabrication envelope and
versioned S-parameters.
RF-03 - Feed budgets. From radio package launch to the declared antenna-feed plane, target no more
than 1.5 dB total dissipative/mismatch loss per path, at least 10 dB return loss, and no more than 0.75 dB
amplitude imbalance between equivalent paths. Target no more than 5 degrees residual channel phase
error after calibration. A two-way divider's ideal -3.01 dB split is not dissipative loss; report excess loss
separately. Verify across band, process corners and temperature samples.
RF-04 - Phase and delay. Specify electrical phase and group delay at common de-embedded reference
planes, not only equal physical trace lengths. At 62 GHz and illustrative effective permittivity 2.8, the
guided wavelength is about 2.89 mm; a 5-degree phase error corresponds to only 40 micrometers of
electrical path mismatch. Include bends, launches, vias, coupling and dielectric variation.
Frequency-dependent calibration cannot repair lost signal power.
RF-05 - Microstrip and stripline. Prefer microstrip or grounded coplanar waveguide for short
millimeter-wave feeds. If stripline is used, include both transitions and its ground structure in the
full-wave model. Add a same-stackup stripline transition coupon as an optional toolkit benchmark; the
radar shall not acquire a long lossy buried feed just to exhibit every primitive.
RF-06 - Manufacturability. Minimum width, gap, island size, via spacing and mask registration shall
come from a quoted process. Use eroded, nominal and dilated geometries and sampled material/process
variations during optimization. Do not retain sub-resolution features because a solver mesh happened to
permit them. Initial acceptance requires a manufacturable design that meets hard masks in declared
bounded corners; no fabricated statistical yield percentage shall be inferred from synthetic corners alone.
RF-07 - Coupling and leakage. Solve the antenna banks, feed region and representative nearby
board/radome structures together. Include embedded-element patterns, active input match, port
coupling and polarization. Require at least 10 dB input headroom below the applicable
compression/blocker limits and at least 6 dB ADC headroom in the declared nearest-target/leakage test.
Require no more than 3 dB measured desensitization from self-leakage at the acceptance ranges. Select
antenna separation/shielding from that budget; an arbitrary isolation figure alone is not a radar
validation.
RF-08 - Measurement access. Provide removable panel coupons and calibrated millimeter-wave probe
or waveguide-fixture access to feed structures. A 6 GHz hobby VNA and SMA coupon cannot validate a 60
GHz product. Require a booked measurement path covering the product band before G2. Bench
equipment is development cost and shall not be hidden in the $100 claim.

6. yapnr workflow and acceptance contracts
6.1 Repository state and required extensions
At the inspected snapshot, coupon tooling exists on main, and its documentation explicitly says the
boards have not been ordered or measured. Its supplied predictions cover 0.1-18 GHz, with stated
specifications over 1-12 GHz. Those fits cannot be extrapolated into a validated 60 GHz material model.
[R2]
Draft PR #30 reports that a divider passes its three-grid checks; antenna synthesis, a Wilkinson-type
combiner and other cases remain unresolved. Issue #32 proposes catalog-part/distributed-element
co-simulation and iteration with PnR. These are foundations, not evidence of an already qualified
end-to-end millimeter-wave flow. [R3, R4]
PNR-01 - Declared RF objects. The proposed interface shall represent a copper RF component as
geometry plus ports, reference planes, ground/keepout ownership, an orientation-dependent radiation
model where relevant, fabrication limits and validation artifacts. These are proposed contracts; this
document does not invent an existing yapnr API.
PNR-02 - Hierarchy. Preserve each TX/RX phase-center lattice as a constrained group. Place the radio,
supplies, memory and interfaces with their own electrical/thermal contracts. Route RF connections using
qualified line and transition families. Preserve ground returns and antenna clearances through fill, repair
and final export.
PNR-03 - Coupled iteration. Evaluate compact network models during search; re-extract the changed
RF region after placement/routing; run full-wave sign-off on the final exported copper. Coupled adjacent
blocks require a joint model; blindly cascading independent two-ports misses coupling. Changes to
stackup, ground fill, radome or nearby metal invalidate affected caches.
PNR-04 - Optimization priorities. Reject DRC, connection, fabrication, RF-mask, power and regulatory
violations first. Among feasible candidates, minimize recurring cost, RF excess loss and area, then
runtime. Selection is mechanical and seed-controlled, following the repository's Monte
Carlo/successive-halving policy. An agent shall not hand-select a visually attractive board or manually
route a failing design to claim automatic success. [R1]
PNR-05 - Evidence levels. Label outputs as analytical estimate, solver prediction, synthetic recovery,
measured coupon or measured system. Require mesh refinement, passivity/energy checks, calibrated
material loss and an independent solver or measured comparison. A radiated-fraction objective alone
cannot establish antenna gain: loss and radiation must be distinguished. Report dispersion,
finite-conductivity and roughness limitations of the solver at 60 GHz.
PNR-06 - Reproducibility. Hash the requirements, parts/models, stackup posterior, solver version,
seed, geometry and firmware. Every solver worker has a timeout and resource budget. New engine
behavior remains behind a default-off flag until its declared A/B result passes. Native KiCad DRC must
report zero unconnected items and zero unwaived violations; RF acceptance remains an additional check.
[R1]
6.2 End-to-end sequence
 1. Freeze the operational domain, jurisdiction, part revision and RF ports.
 2. Build the conventional reference and measurement fixtures; characterize same-process coupons.
 3. Fit material/process parameters with uncertainty over the actual product band.
 4. Optimize single radiator, two-way feed and launch primitives against robust masks.
 5. Assemble TX/RX arrays and calculate their coupled embedded patterns.
 6. Place and route the complete board; re-extract changed structures and iterate.
 7. Sign off the exported board, radome, power, memory and waveform constraints together.

7. Spectrum and operating profiles
REG-01 - Initial US profile. Design for 47 CFR 15.255(c)(2)(iii)(A): its radar provisions specify a 14 dBm
peak EIRP ceiling and at least 25.5 ms of qualifying transmitter off-time in every contiguous 33 ms
interval; qualifying continuous off intervals are at least 2 ms. The outdoor fixed/qualifying vehicular
alternative in (B) allows 20 dBm with a different off-time constraint. The baseline targets 12 dBm peak
EIRP for margin. A higher-power outdoor profile is optional and needs its own verification. [R8]
The proposed 4.80 ms burst every 50 ms leaves at least 28.20 ms outside the burst in any 33 ms
window. At most one burst can intersect such a window. Even if a window boundary leaves a silent
fragment shorter than 2 ms and that fragment is excluded, more than 26.20 ms remains, exceeding the
25.5 ms requirement. This conservative calculation treats the entire burst as transmitting and does not
credit short inter-chirp gaps. Firmware shall enforce this envelope, including startup, calibration and
diagnostic emissions; measured timelines and the certification test method must confirm it at G1.
REG-02 - Full-array EIRP. Verify the worst pointing direction, frequency, process and radome condition
for every phase code. Three coherent equal TX ports can add 20 log10(3) = 9.54 dB relative to one port
at the same per-port power and element pattern. The power setting must back off accordingly.
Beamforming improves spatial selectivity; it is not permission to exceed the radiated limit. Factory
calibrations and startup states must obey the same envelope.
REG-03 - Product boundary. Review emissions, exposure, certification, installation conditions and the
rule's restriction on external phase-locking inputs before hardware release. The baseline is one radio IC
with an internal reference and an integral array; it shall not expose an external synchronization input
intended to aggregate independently certified transmitters. Qualification of this architecture is a G1
regulatory deliverable. [R8, R13]
REG-04 - Regional variants. Do not assume US settings are legal in Europe, Japan or elsewhere. Each
release shall name the country, applicable standard/rule version, waveform, power and installation
category. The 24 GHz fallback requires its own regional review. The high-power 61-61.5 GHz option
trades away bandwidth and is not the default design.

9. Verification gates and stop conditions
G0 - Requirements and radio access. Freeze the domain, reference part, source availability and exact
pricing basis. Verify firmware can acquire the required complex data and control TX phase. Stop if the
chosen part does not expose the required mode or the project can only access a closed antenna module.
G1 - Waveform and implementation proof. Demonstrate chirp/IF feasibility, memory fit, processing
time, three-TX supply behavior, regulatory profile and complete RF-active timeline on a reference
platform. Use the 20-cycle profile and verify the complete rolling-window off-time accounting. Stop if the
system needs an unbudgeted external processor or cannot meet its legal power/timing envelope.
G2 - Material and primitive proof. Measure same-process lines, launches, a divider and a radiator
over the operating band. Require model-to-measurement agreement within 0.5 dB magnitude and 5
degrees transmission phase for the specified feed structures after de-embedding, with resonance centers
within 1%. If not, update the model or process before optimizing the complete radar. Access to
appropriate metrology is a prerequisite.
G3 - Array and receiver proof. Validate coupled antenna patterns, calibrated steering, leakage
headroom, IF response and the reference-target link budget. Run the perception and phased-transmit
modes independently. Recalculate range claims from measured sensitivity and legal EIRP. Stop if the
accepted geometry cannot support the promised angular performance.
G4 - Automatic layout proof. Generate and route complete reference/optimized boards using frozen
contracts and mechanical selection. Require native DRC plus final-copper RF sign-off. Compare runs
across a fixed seed set and fixed compute budgets; report failures as well as the winning candidate. No
hand-edited copper rescue qualifies.
G5 - Built-system and cost proof. Characterize at least ten units across two fabrication lots, including
conventional controls. Run RAD-01 through RAD-10, pattern tests and environmental characterization.
Publish unit/lot variation; ten units do not prove mass-production yield. Close supplier quotes and report
materials, assembly, calibration and development costs separately.
G6 - Demonstration release. Show requirements -> optimization -> full-board PnR -> predicted fields
-> fabricated radar -> calibrated target measurements. Freeze source/configuration hashes and make
datasets reproducible. A full vehicle qualification program begins only after this gate; it is not an implied
deliverable of the demo.
10. Feasibility assessment
Hardware cost: plausible, medium-to-high confidence at concept level. A roughly $19 catalog
radar IC makes a sub-$100 sensor credible without custom silicon. The uncertain cost drivers are RF PCB
yield, packaging and calibration, rather than the transceiver alone.
Useful short-range radar: feasible in principle, medium confidence for these exact targets.
Available bandwidth and coherent reception support the proposed range/Doppler performance.
Link-budget estimates have enough margin for controlled reference targets, but measured leakage and
clutter decide useful performance. Human RCS varies strongly and is not guaranteed by the reflector
result.
Phased transmit steering: feasible but modest. Three close-spaced TX phase centers can visibly
steer a broad beam. They do not produce a pencil beam. The baseline intentionally accepts coarse
angular separation; larger arrays or more channels would need another geometry and cost study.
End-to-end inverse design and automatic PnR: the largest project risk. The inspected
implementation is still developing the required primitives, and current coupons are below the proposed
band. At 60 GHz, etch, roughness, ground transitions, material dispersion and coupled radiation are
first-order effects. Meeting this document requires new validation work, not simply retuning a 5.8 GHz
example.
